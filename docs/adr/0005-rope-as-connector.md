# ADR 0005 — Rope as a connector: welds, knots, and the token round-trip

- **Date:** 2026-10-21
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Scope:** Issue 04 (rope as a connector). Covers `tests/bucket.test.js`, `tests/rope-connector.test.js`, `tests/connector-reset.test.js`, `tests/rope-snap-hints.test.js` — the feature's executable contracts.

> **Superseded in part (2026-10-23) by ADR 0007** (`docs/adr/0007-rope-connection-redesign.md`), the rope connection redesign: the connection-UX decisions of this record — tying via drop-snap and pull-to-tie proximity, snap/attract hint visuals, endpoint-only knot addressing, clamped-drop tying, and sever-as-total-demolition — are replaced by explicit click-to-connect via squares, segment-indexed knots, fixed-length/unlimited-span types with beyond-reach refusal, wholesale drag-untie, and piece severing. The engine-link realize-per-step seam, the ordinal-token round-trip and two-phase bind, snapshot rewind convergence, the attachment-point contract, the welded-bucket construction, and the chain-rest-length corrections stand unchanged.

## Context

Issue 04 makes two machine structures real. First, buckets must be *movable game pieces*: hoisted, lowered or dropped by ropes and payloads (goal buckets stay fixed). Second, a **placed rope must be able to tie its two endpoints to other objects at placement time** — and the tie must survive play/reset cycles, release payloads when the rope severs, and rewind correctly across snapshots.

Constraints inherited from the codebase: rope chains are object-owned (ADR 0004), the engine exposes the runtime-link seam `attachConstraint/detachConstraint` (ADR 0004), object lifecycle is create → play (`onBeforeUpdate`) → restore (`restoreState`) → dispose (ADR 0003), and the board snapshot/restore flow lives in `js/reset/levelReset.js`. Objects carry their `isFixed` flag from level data or placement, consumed by `BaseObject` and never part of behavior options (C3 round-trip).

## Decision

1. **Movable bucket = welded bodies, not a compound.** `Bucket` builds its left wall / right wall / floor / sensor as four *dynamic* bodies by default, held by four distance welds (`stiffness: 1`, lengths from the construction layout: wall↔wall↔floor triangle plus the sensor pinned to the floor). Rationale: the engine's collision mapping and interaction dispatch key on *part* bodies (`_bodyToObject` is per body), so a Matter compound parent would break catch routing; four welded bodies keep everything part-keyed and preserve the interaction-row paths unchanged. A **fixed** bucket builds zero welds and static bodies — goal buckets behave exactly as before. Welds are exposed via the `constraints` getter, so `PhysicsEngine.addObject/removeObject` (which iterate `gameObject.constraints` generically) add and remove them wholesale with zero engine changes. `Bucket.draw` already reads body positions, so a moving bucket renders correctly.
2. **Rope endpoints are: start anchor (static) and the free last segment.** Endpoint 0's tying body is `_anchor`; endpoint 1's is `_segments[last]`. There is no end anchor body. `Rope.setEndpoints(start, end)` re-lays the whole rope between two points (edit-time placement seam — the rope must be out of every physics world when called) and rebuilds the chain for the new span, preserving the original slack ratios (retired in decision 12 — chain rest lengths now equal the laid layout).
3. **Knots are live refs; the physics is engine-owned links realized per step (ADR 0004).** `Rope` keeps `_attachments[endpoint]` as plain lists of `{ target, pointId }` — several knots may share an endpoint, and one attachment point may be shared by several ropes. Each step, `Rope.onBeforeUpdate` reconciles: prune dead knots (target popped / disposed / no longer registered on that engine), drop engine links at the rope's endpoint bodies that no longer match a knot, and realize missing links via `physics.attachConstraint(linkBody, targetBody, { pointB: ap.offset, length: 0, stiffness: 0.9, damping: 0.1 })`. Realization is lazy (first step after place/bind), idempotent, and rides the snapshot seam for free. A severed rope realizes nothing (see 4). Untying (`clearAttachments`) drops the physical links on the next step; DragDrop unties a rope when the player *moves* it.
4. **Severing releases tied payloads (co-termination).** The engine's `rope:severed` consumer (`_detachRopeConstraints`) now also calls `_detachConstraintsTouching(ropeBodyIds)`: the moment the chain parts, every engine-attached link at any rope body dies, and the freed segment keeps standing in the world to fall — the static anchor stays as a nail; the payload is free the same beat.
5. **Attachment tokens live only in serialized data.** `Rope.serialize()` emits no knot data. The board annotator `levelReset.serializePlacedBoard(board)` (used by `beginPlay`) injects, on each rope's row, `attachments: [{ endpoint, targetType, targetOrdinal, pointId }]` — the **ordinal indexes the full board snapshot array** (fixed objects first from level JSON, placed parts after; stable order across play/reset cycles because `_setupLevel` rebuilds fixed objects before placed rows are restored). Deserialization is *passive*: `Rope` accepts a top-level `attachments` option into `_pendingAttachments` (consumed in the constructor, never round-tripped through `_options`); `restorePlacedFromSnapshot` binds **two-phase** — after every row is deserialized, ropes resolve ordinals against the rebuilt board. Guards: total row/board count disagreement → bind everything to nothing with a warning; an out-of-range/mistyped/unbindable token → dropped with a warning; a severed rope never binds tokens.
6. **Snapshot rewind convergence for same-instance restore.** `Rope.restoreState(data)` converges the live rope to the snapshot's severed flag: matching → no-op (chain identity preserved); severed-at-snapshot + intact-live → `sever()`; intact-at-snapshot + severed-live → `this._severed = false`, rebuild the chain, and emit **`rope:chain-converge { rope, previous }`** — a new engine consumer (`_restoreRopeConstraints`) re-adds the rope-owned chain to the world (a registered rope never sees `addObject` again), guarded so a rope living in *another* engine cannot leak its chain across the global bus. The `severed` option round-trips through serialize/deserialize (preserving the "no resurrection" contract from `rope.test.js`), and payload links re-materialize either from the snapshot's captured link records (ADR 0004) or by the next reconcile step.
7. **Attachment points are a BaseObject contract.** `attachmentPoints` (default `[]`) returns `{ id, body, offset }` entries — the point rides a specific body in body-local terms; `getAttachmentWorldPoint(id)` resolves it in world space (position + offset, unrotated: all current attachment-bearing objects render upright). Balloon offers `knot` (the drawn triangle: offset `(0, r·1.2 + 4)`; gone when popped); Bucket offers `rim` (the mouth's top edge, floor-local `(0, −(height − wallThickness/2))`). Placement snapping: DragDrop ties a placed rope's endpoint to the nearest board attachment point within 24 px (`ROPE_SNAP_RADIUS`, exported by `Rope.js`), then knots it; the renderer draws a small ring on each knot in edit modes so ties stay visible machine structure, and while a rope hangs on the cursor marks every board attachment point — faint dashed beyond reach, bright green ring + tie-line when a clamped drop would tie (issue 04 placement visibility; tests/rope-snap-hints.test.js).
8. **Bug found and fixed on the way:** with a dynamic sensor, the bucket's sensor and floor now generate their own collision pairs, and the `bucket-catch` row (target: ANY) made a falling bucket *catch itself*. `Bucket.isCatchContact` now rejects self-pairs.
9. **Conformance scenario update:** the pair-conformance `bucket-catch` scenario pins its bucket (`isFixed: true`) — real goal buckets are fixed, and bucket mobility has its own suite; the row's catch-once contract is unchanged.
10. **Tying is order-independent (the object-side mirror).** The placement tie used to fire only while the *rope* was the dragged part, so a placed rope sat inert while its payload was placed. `_snapObjectToRopes` now runs for every non-rope placement: each attachment point of the dropped object (balloon `knot`, bucket `rim`) ties every board rope endpoint — anchor (0) or free end (1) — whose world position is within `ROPE_SNAP_RADIUS`, using the same `attachEndpoint` refs, nearest-wins choice and lazy length-0 / stiffness-0.9 links as rope-side tying; one attachment point may hold several ropes and one endpoint may hold several knots, exactly as in decision 3. Unlike the rope-side drop-snap, the laid rope is **not re-layed**: re-laying could drag existing knots on its other endpoint, and a knot link is length-0, so the ≤ 24 px residual gap closes on the first play step. Deleting a payload leaves a dead ref on the rope (guarded render/serialize, pruned by the first reconcile step); dropping a fresh payload re-ties — the replace flow is just placement again. While a knot-bearing part hangs on the cursor, `Renderer._drawRopeEndpointHints` marks every board rope endpoint the way `_drawRopeSnapHints` marks attachment points: faint dashed beyond reach, bright ring + tie-line when a clamped drop would tie (the hint reads the same clamp the drop applies). Rope ghosts keep the original hints. Rope-to-rope tying stays unsupported: ropes expose no attachment points (`tests/rope-snap-hints.test.js`, 21 tests; suite 22 files / 294 green).
11. **Pull to tie (object-side placement is forgiving and lands exactly).** Playtesting showed the seam's blind spot: `_snapObjectToRopes` wrote a knot only when the drop's attachment point already sat within `ROPE_SNAP_RADIUS` (24 px) of a board rope endpoint — but both the point and the endpoint are invisible to the eye (a bucket's `rim` rides an anchor offset above the visual mouth), so near-miss drops left the rope inert and the payload free-falling; and even an in-radius tie deliberately left a ≤ 24 px residual for the first play step's length-0 link to yank closed. The seam now *pulls to tie*: the nearest board rope endpoint within **`ROPE_ATTRACT_RADIUS` (64 px)** translates the whole fresh drop (`setPosition`, boundary re-clamped) so its attachment point lands exactly on that endpoint, and every endpoint inside `ROPE_SNAP_RADIUS` still ties in place besides the winner — the pull is one rigid translation, so other candidates' endpoint distances are preserved and nothing already in tie range is dragged out. The rope side is still never re-layed, and the play-start yank is eliminated rather than deferred (decision 10's "residual closes on the first play step" clause now describes only what decision 11's pull replaced); `Rope._reconcileAttachments` realizes every link unchanged. `Renderer._drawRopeEndpointHints` glows by the same rule — bright = within tie range, or = the one endpoint the drop gets pulled onto — so green keeps meaning yes (`tests/rope-snap-hints.test.js`, 25 tests; suite 27 files / 318 green).
12. **Physics corrections from the bucket-on-rope bug file (`bug-20261023T152411Z-05xt`).** The recorded run surfaced three compounding defects in the decision 1/2 construction, fixed together. (a) The chain's "slack ratios" (decision 2: anchor link `0.5 × segLength`, segment links `0.6 × segLength`) gave the chain a rest span ~58% of its laid layout — every play started with a violent inboard contraction yank, and the chain stayed permanently over-tensioned. Segment-link rest lengths now equal the laid spacing (`segLength`; the anchor link keeps `0.5 × segLength`), so the intact chain rests at the laid span (regression: `tests/rope.test.js` "builds chain links that rest at the laid spacing"). (b) The four welded parts sit in flush contact by construction — the walls' inner faces exactly on the floor's ends, the sensor's bottom exactly on the floor's top — and under the default collision filter Matter resolved those self-pairs every step; with only centre-held welds each self-contact resolved into off-centre nudges, so the walls spun (±513° over the run), the container folded, and the hanging bucket flipped mouth-down while still satisfying every weld and the rim knot (21 self-collision events in the recording). Each bucket now builds one **exclusive negative collision group** (`Body.nextGroup(true)`, skipping the rope chain's reserved −1) shared by its four parts: intra-bucket pairs never resolve, groups differ across buckets, and against everything else the filter falls through to the default category masks. (c) One centre-to-centre distance weld per pair holds positions but leaves all four parts free to rotate — self-contact nudges spun the parts rather than separating them. The wall↔floor and sensor↔floor joints are now **two zero-length seam-end pins each** (both ends of the parts' shared contact seam; two distinct coinciding points lock the pair's relative rotation), the wall↔wall rim weld keeps its centre distance to triangulate the mouth, and every weld length is satisfied exactly by the construction layout so no weld starts under load — 7 weld constraints total (`tests/bucket.test.js` updated, plus a free-fall no-torque assertion). (d) `Bucket.draw` keyed its frame to the left wall's position only, so the render could hang untied from the physics pose (the recorded flip left the drawn bucket ~50 px below the rope end); it now draws in the **floor body's frame** (position + angle — the same body the `rim` tie point rides), matching the construction origin pixel-for-pixel upright and rotating with the container. Post-fix replay of the bug file: 0 bucket self-collisions, wall spin ≤ 0.01°, the formation upright through the whole run, knot holding at the rim.

## Consequences

- `EditorPanel._saveLevel` still fails to write placed rows (pre-existing): annotation-based persistence therefore rides the **per-play** placed snapshot only (beginPlay → reset). The annotator lives in the shared reset module so a placed-rows save fix inherits it; noted as follow-up on the tracker.
- Matter pins worth knowing: `Constraint.solve` re-rotates `pointA/pointB` **in place** as bodies rotate (a knot link's stored `pointB` follows the body's rotation; snapshots capture the create-time offset and restore replays an equivalent link); `Constraint.create` with explicit `length: 0` pins the knot point coincident with the rope body.
- A balloon tied through its knot strains visibly against a static pin (buoyancy vs. the taut link) and lifts little else — balloon lift is ~1.5× its own weight, below a ball's. Level-04 needs no hoisting: its hint/description were rewritten truthfully (the ramp is fixed and cannot be lifted; the win path is ball-into-the-floating-bucket). Ball-tetherability as a deeper level-design question is flagged on the tracker.
- The knot reconcile is O(links × knots × endpoints) per rope per step — negligible at puzzle scale.
