# ADR 0005 — Rope as a connector: welds, knots, and the token round-trip

- **Date:** 2026-10-21
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Scope:** Issue 04 (rope as a connector). Covers `tests/bucket.test.js`, `tests/rope-connector.test.js`, `tests/connector-reset.test.js` — the feature's executable contracts.

## Context

Issue 04 makes two machine structures real. First, buckets must be *movable game pieces*: hoisted, lowered or dropped by ropes and payloads (goal buckets stay fixed). Second, a **placed rope must be able to tie its two endpoints to other objects at placement time** — and the tie must survive play/reset cycles, release payloads when the rope severs, and rewind correctly across snapshots.

Constraints inherited from the codebase: rope chains are object-owned (ADR 0004), the engine exposes the runtime-link seam `attachConstraint/detachConstraint` (ADR 0004), object lifecycle is create → play (`onBeforeUpdate`) → restore (`restoreState`) → dispose (ADR 0003), and the board snapshot/restore flow lives in `js/reset/levelReset.js`. Objects carry their `isFixed` flag from level data or placement, consumed by `BaseObject` and never part of behavior options (C3 round-trip).

## Decision

1. **Movable bucket = welded bodies, not a compound.** `Bucket` builds its left wall / right wall / floor / sensor as four *dynamic* bodies by default, held by four distance welds (`stiffness: 1`, lengths from the construction layout: wall↔wall↔floor triangle plus the sensor pinned to the floor). Rationale: the engine's collision mapping and interaction dispatch key on *part* bodies (`_bodyToObject` is per body), so a Matter compound parent would break catch routing; four welded bodies keep everything part-keyed and preserve the interaction-row paths unchanged. A **fixed** bucket builds zero welds and static bodies — goal buckets behave exactly as before. Welds are exposed via the `constraints` getter, so `PhysicsEngine.addObject/removeObject` (which iterate `gameObject.constraints` generically) add and remove them wholesale with zero engine changes. `Bucket.draw` already reads body positions, so a moving bucket renders correctly.
2. **Rope endpoints are: start anchor (static) and the free last segment.** Endpoint 0's tying body is `_anchor`; endpoint 1's is `_segments[last]`. There is no end anchor body. `Rope.setEndpoints(start, end)` re-lays the whole rope between two points (edit-time placement seam — the rope must be out of every physics world when called) and rebuilds the chain for the new span, preserving the original slack ratios.
3. **Knots are live refs; the physics is engine-owned links realized per step (ADR 0004).** `Rope` keeps `_attachments[endpoint]` as plain lists of `{ target, pointId }` — several knots may share an endpoint, and one attachment point may be shared by several ropes. Each step, `Rope.onBeforeUpdate` reconciles: prune dead knots (target popped / disposed / no longer registered on that engine), drop engine links at the rope's endpoint bodies that no longer match a knot, and realize missing links via `physics.attachConstraint(linkBody, targetBody, { pointB: ap.offset, length: 0, stiffness: 0.9, damping: 0.1 })`. Realization is lazy (first step after place/bind), idempotent, and rides the snapshot seam for free. A severed rope realizes nothing (see 4). Untying (`clearAttachments`) drops the physical links on the next step; DragDrop unties a rope when the player *moves* it.
4. **Severing releases tied payloads (co-termination).** The engine's `rope:severed` consumer (`_detachRopeConstraints`) now also calls `_detachConstraintsTouching(ropeBodyIds)`: the moment the chain parts, every engine-attached link at any rope body dies, and the freed segment keeps standing in the world to fall — the static anchor stays as a nail; the payload is free the same beat.
5. **Attachment tokens live only in serialized data.** `Rope.serialize()` emits no knot data. The board annotator `levelReset.serializePlacedBoard(board)` (used by `beginPlay`) injects, on each rope's row, `attachments: [{ endpoint, targetType, targetOrdinal, pointId }]` — the **ordinal indexes the full board snapshot array** (fixed objects first from level JSON, placed parts after; stable order across play/reset cycles because `_setupLevel` rebuilds fixed objects before placed rows are restored). Deserialization is *passive*: `Rope` accepts a top-level `attachments` option into `_pendingAttachments` (consumed in the constructor, never round-tripped through `_options`); `restorePlacedFromSnapshot` binds **two-phase** — after every row is deserialized, ropes resolve ordinals against the rebuilt board. Guards: total row/board count disagreement → bind everything to nothing with a warning; an out-of-range/mistyped/unbindable token → dropped with a warning; a severed rope never binds tokens.
6. **Snapshot rewind convergence for same-instance restore.** `Rope.restoreState(data)` converges the live rope to the snapshot's severed flag: matching → no-op (chain identity preserved); severed-at-snapshot + intact-live → `sever()`; intact-at-snapshot + severed-live → `this._severed = false`, rebuild the chain, and emit **`rope:chain-converge { rope, previous }`** — a new engine consumer (`_restoreRopeConstraints`) re-adds the rope-owned chain to the world (a registered rope never sees `addObject` again), guarded so a rope living in *another* engine cannot leak its chain across the global bus. The `severed` option round-trips through serialize/deserialize (preserving the "no resurrection" contract from `rope.test.js`), and payload links re-materialize either from the snapshot's captured link records (ADR 0004) or by the next reconcile step.
7. **Attachment points are a BaseObject contract.** `attachmentPoints` (default `[]`) returns `{ id, body, offset }` entries — the point rides a specific body in body-local terms; `getAttachmentWorldPoint(id)` resolves it in world space (position + offset, unrotated: all current attachment-bearing objects render upright). Balloon offers `knot` (the drawn triangle: offset `(0, r·1.2 + 4)`; gone when popped); Bucket offers `rim` (the mouth's top edge, floor-local `(0, −(height − wallThickness/2))`). Placement snapping: DragDrop ties a placed rope's endpoint to the nearest board attachment point within 24 px, then knots it; the renderer draws a small ring on each knot in edit modes so ties stay visible machine structure.
8. **Bug found and fixed on the way:** with a dynamic sensor, the bucket's sensor and floor now generate their own collision pairs, and the `bucket-catch` row (target: ANY) made a falling bucket *catch itself*. `Bucket.isCatchContact` now rejects self-pairs.
9. **Conformance scenario update:** the pair-conformance `bucket-catch` scenario pins its bucket (`isFixed: true`) — real goal buckets are fixed, and bucket mobility has its own suite; the row's catch-once contract is unchanged.

## Consequences

- `EditorPanel._saveLevel` still fails to write placed rows (pre-existing): annotation-based persistence therefore rides the **per-play** placed snapshot only (beginPlay → reset). The annotator lives in the shared reset module so a placed-rows save fix inherits it; noted as follow-up on the tracker.
- Matter pins worth knowing: `Constraint.solve` re-rotates `pointA/pointB` **in place** as bodies rotate (a knot link's stored `pointB` follows the body's rotation; snapshots capture the create-time offset and restore replays an equivalent link); `Constraint.create` with explicit `length: 0` pins the knot point coincident with the rope body.
- A balloon tied through its knot strains visibly against a static pin (buoyancy vs. the taut link) and lifts little else — balloon lift is ~1.5× its own weight, below a ball's. Level-04 needs no hoisting: its hint/description were rewritten truthfully (the ramp is fixed and cannot be lifted; the win path is ball-into-the-floating-bucket). Ball-tetherability as a deeper level-design question is flagged on the tracker.
- The knot reconcile is O(links × knots × endpoints) per rope per step — negligible at puzzle scale.
