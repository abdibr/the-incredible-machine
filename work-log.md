## 2026-10-23 — Pinned rope: adjustable fixed length with beyond-reach refusal (issue #54, ADR 0007 decision 4)

The pinned rope gets its length from the hand, as decided: a fresh drop hangs 100 px straight down from its anchor; while the rope is untied, a seek at its free end reels the rope to the cursor continuously (the tie square rides the cursor); the first tie freezes the length; a tie click aimed beyond the rope's reach is refused with feedback — the seeking handle flashes red and a "rope too short" badge marks the refused aim — instead of silently snapping or stretching; tied, the rope keeps sagging as a slack chain (max length with slack, never a rigid rod).

- **Reach semantics**: a pinned rope's chain capacity is its rest-length sum — `(SEGMENTS − 0.5)/SEGMENTS × length` (≈ 0.917·L), because the tie body is the last segment's **center**, half a segment inboard of the layout end (ADR 0005 decision 12: rest lengths equal the laid distances). The predicate `Rope.isPointWithinReach(x, y, slack = 0)` measures exactly that from the static anchor and is the single source both `getTieSquares`' glow and the tie gate use (with the click's 14px aim tolerance) — glow = tieable, exact. The endpoint-0 knot realizes **at the anchor itself** (it consumes no chain), so the same uniform measure gates both ends: a pinned rope can only tie to what lies within a rope-length of its pin.
- **Re-lay (`Rope.relayFreeEnd(x, y)`, new)**: lays the chain so the free-end tie square lands exactly on the demand — the chain spans demand · `SEGMENTS/(SEGMENTS − 0.5)` — with the span floored at the minimum viable span `MIN_ROPE_SPAN = 48` (= SEGMENTS × 8px pitch: six 3px segments stay a chain, never collapse) instead of breaking the chain, always along the demanded direction. `Rope.setEndpoints` itself stays **exact** (the drop-time snap re-lays endpoints exactly onto their matches — pinned by `rope-snap-hints.test.js`, "the free end's re-lay position is the rope-chain layout's business") but now announces `rope:chain-converge { rope, previous }` when it rebuilds the chain; the engine consumer (`PhysicsEngine._restoreRopeConstraints`) became a real **swap** (drops `previous`, adds the rebuilt chain, wakes the rope) — an in-world re-lay (post play→reset rewind) can no longer leave stale constraints in the world; for out-of-world ropes the registration guard keeps the event a no-op.
- **Freeze + covering knots**: `Rope.lengthFrozen` is **derived from the knot lists** (it can never drift from the tie state; the last untie — per-knot or the wholesale move untie — hands length back to the hand). A frozen rope's re-lay only re-orients the free end around the frozen reach circle; the length is sealed exactly (`relayFreeEnd` re-asserts it after `setEndpoints`' layout re-derivation, so float dust never compounds). `attachEndpoint` **grows** the fixed length when a free-end tie reaches past the laid capacity (tied span × 6/5.5), so the chain is never over-tensioned by its own knot, and no tie ever shrinks the rope. `Rope.serialize()` emits `options.lengthFrozen` (informational — restore re-derives it when the token bind re-ties).
- **Tie/cancel precedence in the seek**: the tie aim now wins the seat over the re-click-cancel when both sit inside the 14px hit radius — necessary because the re-lay moves the tie square under the cursor (otherwise dragging the end to a target and clicking it would cancel instead of tie). A plain re-click on the square (no target in radius) still cancels; Esc still cancels; a click that ties nothing is still swallowed.
- **Refusal feedback**: `ConnectFlow._refuse(x, y)` / `refusal` (self-expires on read) + `REFUSAL_FLASH_MS = 600` + `ROPE_TOO_SHORT = 'rope too short'`; the renderer flashes the seeking square red (`rgba(232, 76, 76, 0.9)` / `#e34e4e`) and paints the badge at the refused aim; any seek exit (tie, cancel, Esc, stage change, fresh seek) sweeps it. A refused click is still **consumed** by the seek — no DDM selection falls through, the seek survives.
- **Glow rule (the #52 carry-over, resolved)**: `ConnectFlow.getTieSquares` now glows a target **iff the seeking rope reaches it** (`isPointWithinReach` + aim tolerance) — out-of-reach targets stay visible but faint during a seek, and their tie clicks refuse instead of silently snapping.
- **Tests**: `tests/pinned-rope-length.test.js` (new, 11 tests — fresh hang incl. the registry route; `MIN_ROPE_SPAN`; `relayFreeEnd` clamps (untied min-span + frozen circle); reach predicate; reel-to-cursor then tie freezes; slack stays; knot-covering grow; anchor-side tie consumes no chain; snapshot round-trip of length **and** frozen state; in-world chain swap; no out-of-world leak). `tests/connect-flow.test.js` +6 new (anchor seek does not re-lay; the frozen free-end ride; the beyond-reach refusal + its recovery via the reel; refusal decay/cancel sweep; the refused-click DDM consumption; the renderer refusal paint) and updated where the reach rule legitimately reframed #52's "every target glows" to "**in-reach** targets glow" — the affected tests re-aim the hand first (tie = reel, then click). Full suite **35 files / 394 tests, all green** (two unrelated files flaked to 5s timeouts under a concurrent background run and pass in isolation).
- **Status**: proximity surfaces (`_snapRopeEndpoints`, `_snapObjectToRopes`, hint renderers) untouched — their ties stay zero-gap/pull-to-tie, so they remain within reach; the removal is still #53. #55–#59 (free rope, segment-indexed knots, rope-to-rope, severing visuals, tooltips) ride on from here.

## 2026-10-23 — Hardening: ramp surface material restored (trampoline follow-up)

The general follow-up from the trampoline fix (`43486d4`): `js/objects/Ramp.js` declared a static body's material (`friction 0.5 / restitution 0.1`) that matter-js's static flip silently zeroed (→ 1 / 0). For today's population it is behaviorally inert — pairs resolve at `max(live restitutions)` / `min(live frictions)`, every ball variant (restitution ≥ 0.3, friction ≤ 0.5) dominates or washes out the blend, and even a domino resting on a ramp only shifts pair friction 0.6 → 0.5 — but it was a landmine: any future tuning of the ramp's surface would silently do nothing, exactly as nearly happened with the trampoline's 0.95. The ramp now re-asserts its declared material right after body creation, same pattern as Trampoline.js, via named constants `RAMP_FRICTION` / `RAMP_RESTITUTION` with the rationale documented at the constants.

- `tests/ramp.test.js` (new, 2 tests): declared friction 0.5 / restitution 0.1 survive the implicit static flip; and a dead ball (restitution forced to 0 — no shipped variant can exercise the ramp's own 0.1 through the pair's `max()`) dropped on a flat ramp rebounds at ≈ −0.57 vy after an 8.2 vy impact (pair restitution 0.1, ~0.9 px hop) where the pre-fix zeroed material measured exactly 0 (calibration: `.scratch/probe-ramp.mjs`). Full suite: **33 files / 356 tests, all green** (`cmd /c npm test`).
- ADR 0006 invariants intact: the committed fixture `tests/fixtures/bugfiles/ball-drop-ramp.json` still replays **bit-exactly** — the recording's bowling ball (restitution 0.3 > the ramp's 0.1, friction 0.4 < 0.5) dominates or washes out both pair blends either way — and the trampoline/spring regression suites are untouched.

## 2026-10-23 — Bugfix: trampoline fabric had no bounce; spring never launched its rider (two bug files)

Two user-recorded sandbox sessions (ADR 0006 bug files) exposed physics objects whose runtime behavior didn't match what the code declares.

- **Trampoline (bug-20261023T174706Z-0oca)** — *"trampoline has no bounce .. a heavy ball should bounce high from a trampoline"*. matter-js `Body.setStatic()` zeroes a static body's restitution (and forces friction 1), and the `isStatic: true` *option* flows through that same setter inside `Body.create` — so the fabric's declared restitution 0.95 / friction 0.3 were silently discarded. ## 2026-10-21 — Session bug-file recorder + bit-exact replay harness (ADR 0006)

## 2026-10-19 — Rope tying made visible (issue 04 placement UX)

The placement-time rope tying shipped in `833fe4c` (ADR 0005 decision 7) was real but invisible: the 24 px snap was silent, no drag-time markers existed, and the post-drop ring was a hair-thin 5 px circle — a player had no way to see tying happen or aim for it.

- **Drag-time snap hints** (`Renderer._drawRopeSnapHints`, driven by a live drag-state window the renderer reads each frame): while a tool-ghost rope hangs on the cursor, every board attachment point renders — a faint dashed marker when beyond reach, and a bright marker with a tie-line when a clamped drop of the current mouse position would tie (ghost endpoint within radius of the point). - **One constant, two consumers:** `ROPE_SNAP_RADIUS` moved to a `Rope.js` export; `DragDropManager` imports it instead of carrying its own copy (no import cycle — hints render straight off DragDropManager's drag state).

- New `tests/rope-snap-hints.test.js` (9 tests): the drop-tie seam in DragDropManager (tie on a 22 px drop, out-of-range 40 px stays free, nearest-wins, re-lay after anchor ties) + hint rendering (ghost-rope gating, bright-within/faint-beyond, knot ring, hint suppression in play modes) + the strengthened knot ring. ## 2026-10-21 — Rope tying made order-independent (issue 04, object-side mirror)

The placement tie fired only while the rope was the dragged part: a placed rope's endpoints sat inert when a balloon or bucket was dropped onto them, and a popped balloon forced re-laying the whole rope.

- **DragDropManager._snapObjectToRopes** — runs for every non-rope placement: each attachment point of the dropped object ties every board rope endpoint (anchor or free end) within `ROPE_SNAP_RADIUS`, via the same `attachEndpoint` knot refs, nearest-wins and lazy length-0/0.9 links as rope-side tying. The laid rope is deliberately **not re-layed** (re-laying could drag existing knots on its other endpoint); the ≤ 24 px residual closes on the first play step. Delete-then-replace just works: the dead ref is pruned by the first reconcile step and cannot block the fresh tie.
- **Renderer._drawRopeEndpointHints** — the object-side mirror of `_drawRopeSnapHints`: while a knot-bearing part hangs on the cursor, every board rope endpoint renders faint-dashed beyond reach or bright green + tie-line when the drop would tie (same clamped-position math as the tie seam). Severed/disposed ropes are skipped; rope ghosts are unchanged (they keep `_drawRopeSnapHints`).
- **Toolbox copy:** the rope description is now order-neutral ("drop either part right up close to the other").
- Rope-to-rope tying stays intentionally unsupported (ropes expose no attachment points) — decision 10 documents it.
- `tests/rope-snap-hints.test.js` 9 → 21 tests: object-side tie to free end / anchor / bucket rim, out-of-range no-op, severed skip, one knot holding two ropes, replace-after-delete, first-play-step engine-link realization; hints: bright + tie-line, faint dashed, mixed styles on one rope, severed skip. Full suite: **22 files / 294 tests, all green** (`cmd /c npm test`). ## 2026-10-23 — Free-rope knot-follow regression recalibrated + distinct toolbox icon (issue #55)

The knot-follow regression ("the knot travels with its tied target — no fixed anchor") kept failing at 17.6–19.5px against its >20px@40-ticks threshold; earlier "green" recordings came from an unreliable shell that fabricated passing output, so it was in fact never green. A probe (`.scratch/probe-knot-follow.mjs`, an exact mirror of the test setup plus a lone-balloon control) shows the mechanism was never the problem — the threshold was calibrated on lift the buoyancy tuning never supported:

- **Measured statics:** the spawned chain — 6×(r=3, ρ=0.001) segments plus the r=4 dynamic anchor — totals ≈0.2057 mass against the balloon's net lift (buoyancy 0.0015·m up vs own gravity 0.001·m down ⇒ ≈0.00015 net for m≈0.2992). The tied assembly therefore DRIFTS DOWN at a ~1px/tick terminal velocity (17.4–22.2px at t=40 — exactly the failing range). A lone balloon rises ~122px over 40 ticks to its ceiling rest, unchanged. ADR 0007 decision 5 pins the HAUL, not the direction: a static nail holds its spawn point forever in either direction, while the hauled knot ships with the assembly, riding the realized length-0/0.9 link the whole way (gap ≤3.4px, mostly ≤1px); no segment ever dozes (motion stays above sleep slop).
- **Recalibration (test-only, no production change):** horizon 40 → 240 ticks, threshold >20px → >100px — measured drift 207.18–207.29px across three randomized bob-phase runs, while a static nail stays ≈0 regardless of horizon; the <6px ride assertion unchanged and still holding at t=240. The test comment now documents the sink statics so the direction never reads as a bug.
- **Distinct toolbox icon:** `FreeRope.drawToolboxIcon` (`js/objects/registerAllObjects.js`) draws the coiled bundle — three concentric rings mirroring the on-board glyph's radii plus a loose end, never the gray nail — replacing the pinned-rope strand icon the `FreeRope extends Rope` subclass inherited. Verified live at :5173 (toolbox canvases pixel-distinct: hollow center + ring ink vs the strand's 102 ink px; 0 console errors).
- `tests/free-rope.test.js` — 14 tests (the 13 lifecycle contracts + the recalibrated knot-follow); new `tests/free-rope-icon.test.js` — 3 tests (prototype override ≠ inherited icon; free-rope icon records 3 arcs / 0 fills; the pinned baseline still draws its nail). Full suite: **38 files / 402 tests, all green** (`cmd /c npx vitest run`).
